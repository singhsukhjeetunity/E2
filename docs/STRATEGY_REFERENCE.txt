STRATEGY REFERENCE - original instruments and baseline settings

Both EAs use the selected tester/chart symbol. Instrument names are
not eligibility filters. These are original research settings, not evidence
of profitability on other markets. Strategy names in exports remain stable;
the symbol column identifies the actual instrument tested.

Pip convention: 10 quote points for 3/5 digits, otherwise 1 quote point.

EMA SESSION BASELINE
Entry window: 10:00-15:29 New York; half-day cutoff 12:30.
Scheduled exit: 15:55 New York; half days 12:55.
US market calendar remains part of the strategy on EVERY instrument.
Default spread cap: 4 raw price units; deviation: 0.5 raw price units.
Previous supplied USTEC runs used spread cap 10. Entry delay limit: 5 seconds.
For EURUSD, 0.0001 is one conventional pip; a cap of 4 is NOT 4 pips.
Set spread/deviation appropriately for the instrument before testing.
Automatic warm-up: 30 * max(100, 20 * longest indicator period) + 1440
completed M1 bars (31,440 at defaults). No seed-date input.
Short/unavailable history delays new entries; it does not disable chart attachment.
Calendar supported: 2022-2026. Same EMA rules for tester, demo and real accounts.
Broker session guard: exit 5 minutes before the active broker session ends
if earlier than the scheduled US exit. Editable buffer: 1-120 minutes.
A missed exit by more than 60 seconds invalidates the run; closes keep retrying.
Recompile and rerun to validate this fix against your broker history.

CLOCK / CROSS-MARKET TESTING
Choose a verified historical broker UTC offset and DST policy; no universal
broker offset is assumed. Gold also supports its existing server-time mode.
Keep the original session rules when comparing instruments. Changing a
symbol does not automatically change the session, calendar, or spread cap.
Valid contract specifications, sufficient history, margin, and valid inputs
are still required. Removing a symbol restriction does not bypass these.
Test each EA separately and combine exports with explicit risk allocations.
Use distinct magic numbers for separate deployed instances; these exports
do not enforce portfolio-wide risk limits or a shared one-position rule.
